**Goal.** User asked three questions about Proposition 3.2 (`prop:interior`, the interior-regime closed form in `inst/paper/theory.tex`): (1) does a closed form like it exist for general/continuous X, (2) should we give its EIF there, (3) are there regimes between the finite/correlation regime and the general/continuous-functional regime.

Consulted Oracle for a rigorous take before committing to any claims. Oracle's answers: (1) no — the interior regime is empty for any P0 with a continuous component (a measure-theoretic fact, not a representation-theory obstruction as I'd first guessed), so there's no direct analog; the honest continuous-X limit is via an equal-P0-mass sieve. Bonus finding: the same closed form recurs at the *saturated* extreme of lambda (whole-simplex case via Dirichlet(1,...,1) exchangeability), contradicting the paper's "two regimes" framing — it's three. (3) an "effective rank of the reweighting-covariance kernel" theorem unifies finite-K (Prop 3.2 is the rank-(K-1) endpoint) and continuous-X (elbow-bound, full-rank) as two endpoints of one spectral decomposition — the strongest remaining theoretical contribution, but large.
