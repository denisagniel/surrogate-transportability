## 09:56 — Literature scan: Rosa (2026), "Semiparametric Bernstein–von Mises theorems from Stein's method" (arXiv:2609.37939) — relevance to this project

**Context.** Reviewed the paper on request (not part of this project's active work
queue — a general lit-scan across the RAND/rprojects workspace flagged it, then this
project specifically for a closer read). No code or manuscript changes made; this is
a findings note for future reference, e.g. when writing `theory.tex`'s discussion of
Assumption A5 or scoping citations.

**What the paper does.** Proves a Bayesian nonparametric Bernstein–von Mises (BvM)
result: the posterior of a functional concentrates around the semiparametric-efficient
Gaussian limit. Novelty is the proof technique — Stein's method + an
information-geometric framing, replacing the usual Laplace-transform/integrated-
likelihood argument — which yields **explicit non-asymptotic bounds** on the
bounded-Lipschitz distance between the posterior and its Gaussian limit, built from
local differential quantities tied to the efficient influence function (EIF). Worked
applications: quadratic functionals (squared-norm) in Gaussian white-noise models
(§3.1), and linear/quadratic/general integral (bilinear) functionals in histogram
density models, conjugate and non-conjugate priors (§3.2). For linear/quadratic
functionals the same machinery isolates the exact differential term causing posterior
bias and removes it via a functional correction, recovering a BvM theorem in regimes
where the uncorrected functional's would fail.

**Why this project, specifically, is the tightest match found in the workspace.** A
broader scan flagged three frequentist AIPW/EIF causal-effect projects
(`global-scholars/doubletree`, `opioids/.../paper2-methods`,
`smi/.../vector-incremental-effects`) as superficial keyword matches — same
vocabulary (EIF, semiparametric efficiency, cross-fitting) but a different object
(debiasing a treatment-effect point estimator). This project is a materially closer
match on the *object*, not just the vocabulary:

- This project's estimand $\Theta_{ab}=\iint C(x,x')\,\tau_a(x)\tau_b(x')\,d\mathbb P_0(x)\,d\mathbb P_0(x')$
  (`theory.tex` §"General theory: a bilinear-functional estimand") is exactly a
  **quadratic/bilinear functional of a nonparametric regression** — the same object
  class as Rosa §3.1 (diagonal/quadratic case, $\Theta_{aa}$) and §3.2 (off-diagonal/
  bilinear case, $\Theta_{ab}$, histogram-model general integral functionals).
- Assumption A5 (`theory.tex` L1755, "Bilinear-functional √n-estimability") states the
  smoothness elbow $s_S+s_Y>d/2$ off-diagonal / $s_a>d/4$ diagonal, below which the
  minimax rate degrades to $n^{-4s/(4s+d)}$ and √n-normality fails — citing
  Bickel–Ritov 1988, Birgé–Massart 1995, Robins et al. 2008/2017
  (`memory/general-theory-map.md` L20). This is the *same* regularity-boundary
  phenomenon Rosa's Bayesian BvM result addresses for quadratic/bilinear functionals,
  just proved on the frequentist (minimax) side rather than the Bayesian (posterior
  concentration) side.
- Lemma "A5: bilinear remainder and the elbow" (`theory.tex` L1989) already isolates
  the exact leading-order bias term below the elbow and its exact rate — structurally
  the same move Rosa's §2.2/§3.2 makes to isolate the bias-generating differential
  term and correct it via the functional correction.

**Caveat — this is an object match, not a theorem transfer.** Rosa's result is about
posterior concentration under a prior; this project's estimator is a frequentist
AIPW/hit-and-run construction with asymptotic linearity. Nothing in Rosa is a citable
plug-in result for this project's proofs. What transfers is (a) confirmation that the
$s_S+s_Y>d/2$-type elbow is a fundamental limit crossing both paradigms, useful as a
discussion-section citation strengthening the claim that A5 is a real boundary and not
a proof-technique artifact, and (b) Rosa's Stein's-method approach to producing
*explicit, non-asymptotic* constants around an EIF-corrected bilinear functional as a
possible model for tightening the remainder-term Lemma beyond its current $o_p(1)$
statement — relevant to the open "formal Proposition with the EIF derived explicitly +
precise A5 threshold statement" deliverable noted in
`memory/general-theory-map.md` L24.

## 14:20 — Two nonasymptotic results added to `theory.tex`

**Context.** User asked to look into whether nonasymptotic (finite-sample) results are

existing exact/deterministic piece (the Lemma "A5: bilinear remainder and the elbow"
Cauchy–Schwarz bound). Identified four candidate nonasymptotic routes; user selected two:
(1) a Doeblin/minorization mixing bound for the TV-ball hit-and-run sampler, (2) a
finite-$n$ AIPW/IF bound converting Theorem general's asymptotic normality into an
explicit Berry–Esseen-type statement. Both are now in `theory.tex`, compiled clean
(56 pages, exit 0, 0 undefined refs/citations), audited by `proof-auditor`, and corrected.

**Result 1 — Uniform ergodicity of the hit-and-run sampler (`lem:uniform-ergodicity`).**
The TV ball $\mathcal S=\Delta^{K-1}\cap\{q:\|q-P_0\|_1\le2\lambda\}$ is a compact convex
polytope; the sampler's transition density has an explicit uniform floor
$\beta\ge\frac2\nu(r/2R)^\nu$ ($r,R$ = explicit inscribed/circumscribed radii in
$\lambda,K,p_{\min}$), giving whole-space minorization and uniform ergodicity
$\|P^t(q,\cdot)-\mu\|_{TV}\le(1-\beta)^t$ — a real theorem discharging the "geometrically
ergodic" clause `ass:geometry` previously only asserted. $\beta$ is exponentially (in
fact, for realistic $p_{\min}\asymp1/K$, super-exponentially) small in $K$, so this is an
*existence* result, explicitly not a burn-in prescription — stated as such, with a
worked numeric example (β≈1.22×10⁻⁴ at λ=0.3,K=5) and an honest scope remark noting the
lemma governs the idealized kernel, not the R implementation's edge-case branches
(degenerate-segment fallback, renormalization, two `next` guards — the latter shown to
suppress *recording*, not just movement, distinct from a simple lazy-kernel model).
New refs: `lovasz1999hitandrun`.

**Result 2 — Finite-sample Berry–Esseen bound (`thm:finite-n`), interior/finite-$\mathcal X$
regime.** New Assumption A9 (`ass:finite-n`, 6 clauses: bounded outcomes/propensity/
weights, realized-nuisance confinement by clipping, cell-mass floor, and — kept
explicitly distinct from `ass:nondegeneracy`'s across-study variance — a sampling-variance
floor $\sigma^2\ge\sigma_{\min}^2$). New Lemma `lem:chi-bounded` (explicit boundedness of
the bilinear score $\chi_{ab}$). New Lemma `lem:D2-bernstein`: an exact von Mises
decomposition showing the cross-fit correction's first-order conditional bias cancels
*algebraically* (not asymptotically) against the plug-in estimator's own first-order bias,
leaving a genuine second-order remainder bounded by Bernstein's inequality per fold.
Assembled theorem: $\Delta_n=O(n^{-1/2}\log n)$, conditional on the sampled geometry,
oracle-$\sigma$, no Monte Carlo term. Scope remark (`rem:finite-n-scope`) identifies the
*general* continuous-$\mathcal X$ case as blocked not by the Bernstein step but by the
Cauchy–Schwarz remainder itself: $D_1=O(n^{(d-2s)/(2(2s+d))})$ never reaches $n^{-1/2}$ for
finite smoothness, which is why the finite-$n$ theorem is scoped to the parametric-rate
interior regime rather than the general case. New refs: `Shevtsova2011` (Berry–Esseen
constant 0.4690), `BoucheronLugosiMassart2013` (Bernstein's inequality).
